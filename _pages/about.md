<section class="home-introduction" aria-label="About SCALE Lab">
  <p class="home-lead">At Washington State University, SCALE Lab develops physics-grounded models, optimization algorithms, and data-driven methods to inform electric grid operations and planning.</p>
  <p>Our research connects uncertainty in weather, infrastructure, and system conditions to network consequences and practical engineering decisions—from distribution feeders to regional power systems.</p>
  <ul class="home-research-themes">
    <li><strong>Physics-consistent optimization.</strong> Scalable power-flow and network-constrained coordination of grid-edge resources.</li>
    <li><strong>Hazard-dependent vulnerability.</strong> Learning infrastructure risk from weather and outage evidence.</li>
    <li><strong>Resilience and planning decisions.</strong> Evaluating operational responses and infrastructure investments under uncertainty.</li>
  </ul>

<details class="home-director-bio" markdown="1">
  <summary>About the lab director</summary>

**Anamika Dubey (Lab Director)** is Huie-Rogers Endowed Chair Professor of Electrical Engineering at Washington State University, a jointly appointed Research Scientist at Pacific Northwest National Laboratory (PNNL), and Co-Director of the WSU–PNNL Advanced Grid Institute. She received her MSE and Ph.D. in Electrical and Computer Engineering from the University of Texas at Austin in 2012 and 2015. Her research advances physics-consistent optimization, data-driven vulnerability modeling, and risk-informed planning for power systems under uncertainty. Her work has been recognized by the 2023 IEEE PES Outstanding Young Engineer Award and WSU's 2026 VCEA Anjan Bose Outstanding Researcher Award. [Read her CV]({{ '/cv/' | relative_url }}) for details on publications, funding, mentoring, teaching, and professional service.
